Prop. I.26: Circle Area, Lost Archimedean Plinthides & Concentric Annulus
=========================================================================

.. folio:: Schöne 66
   :page: 66

**Proposition I.26 (Circle, Lost Archimedean Bounds & Concentric Annulus, Schöne pp. 66–70)**:

Hero cites :person:`Archimedes' <archimedes>` :cite:`Measurement of a Circle <archimedes:circle>`: the circumference of a circle exceeds thrice its diameter by less than :math:`\frac{1}{7}` and more than :math:`\frac{10}{71}` (:math:`3\frac{10}{71} < \pi < 3\frac{1}{7}`), and the circle's area equals the right triangle of legs :math:`r` and :math:`C`, giving :math:`A_{\text{circle}} \approx \frac{11}{14} d^2`. Crucially, Hero adds a unique citation of Archimedes' otherwise lost treatise :cite:`On Plinthides and Cylinders <archimedes:plinthides>` (*Περὶ πλινθίδων καὶ κυλίνδρων*), recording finer bounds equivalent to:

.. math::

   \frac{211875}{67441} < \pi < \frac{197888}{62351} \quad \text{(with } \frac{211875}{67441} \approx 3.141635\text{)}.

Hero then measures the concentric circular ring (:term:`annulus <itys>`, *ἴτυς*) between outer diameter :math:`D_1` and inner diameter :math:`D_2` of breadth :math:`w = \frac{D_1 - D_2}{2}` by the difference-of-squares identity (:just:`II.5`):

.. math::

   A_{\text{itys}} = \frac{11}{14}\left(D_1^2 - D_2^2\right) = \frac{11}{14} \cdot 4w(D_2 + w).
