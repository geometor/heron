Prop. I.11: Area of an Isosceles Trapezium
==========================================

.. folio:: Schöne 26
   :page: 28

**Proposition I.11 (Isosceles Trapezium, Schöne pp. 28–30)**:

Given parallel bases :math:`6` and :math:`20` and equal oblique legs :math:`13` and :math:`13`, dropping two :term:`perpendiculars <kathetos>` from the upper vertices cuts off equal projections :math:`\frac{20 - 6}{2} = 7`—or in Hero's crisp integer example (:math:`A\Delta = 10, B\Gamma = 20, AB = \Gamma\Delta = 13`), projections of :math:`5`, yielding height :math:`\sqrt{13^2 - 5^2} = 12` and area :math:`\frac{10 + 20}{2} \times 12 = 180`.
