Scholarly Notes — Definitiones, Catoptrica & Dioptra¶

Commentary on Olympiodorus & William of Moerbeke, D’Arcy Thompson’s Gnomon, and the Tunnel of Eupalinus

1. Transmission of Hero’s Catoptrica: Moerbeke & Olympiodorus¶

For centuries, the medieval Latin treatise De speculis—translated from Greek at Viterbo on December 31, 1269 by William of Moerbeke—circulated under the name of Claudius Ptolemy (Ptolemaei De speculis). In 1871–1900, Valentin Rose and Wilhelm Schmidt (Teubner 1900, pp. 301–372) proved decisively that De speculis is in fact Hero of Alexandria’s *Catoptrica*!

The clinching philological proof came from the 6th-century Alexandrian Neoplatonist Olympiodorus, who in his Greek commentary on Aristotle's Meteorology III.2 (Schmidt 1900, pp. 368–372) quotes Hero the Mechanician in his *Catoptrica* (δείκνυσιν Ἥρων ὁ μηχανικὸς ἐν τοῖς αὑτοῦ Κατοπτρικοῖς) and reproduces word-for-word the Greek mirror-reflection proof that matches Moerbeke’s Latin Proposition 4! Thus we possess Hero’s variational shortest-path theorem in authentic Greek prose, directly linking Greek synthetic geometry ([I.4], [I.14], [I.15], [I.20]) to Fermat’s Principle of Least Time (1662), Maupertuis, Euler, and Hamilton’s Principle of Stationary Action. See Shortest-Path Law of Reflection (Catoptrica Prop. 4).

2. Hero’s Definition 58 & D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson’s On Growth and Form¶

Hero’s Definition 58 («καθόλου δὲ γνώμων ἐστὶ πᾶν ὃ προσλαβὸν ὁτιοῦν, ἀριθμὸν ἢ σχῆμα, ποιεῖ τὸ ὅλον ὅμοιον ᾧ προσείληφεν») inspired one of the most celebrated chapters of modern mathematical biology: Chapter XI (“The Equiangular Spiral”) of D’Arcy Wentworth Thompson’s On Growth and Form (1917 / 1942). Whereas Euclid restricted the gnomon to L-shaped parallelogram increments ([II.def.2]), Hero recognized that self-similar accretion unites:

  • Arithmetic Gnomons: Adding the odd number \(2n + 1\) to the square \(n^2\) produces the next square \((n + 1)^2\).

  • Planar Golden Gnomons: Adding a square of side \(1\) to a rectangle of aspect \(1 : (\phi - 1)\) produces a similar rectangle of aspect \(\phi : 1\) (and in isosceles golden triangles of \(36^\circ\text{-}72^\circ\text{-}72^\circ\) and \(108^\circ\text{-}36^\circ\text{-}36^\circ\), adjoining one gnomon triangle produces the next larger similar triangle!).

  • Logarithmic Spirals & Nautilus Shells: Continuous gnomonic accretion around a pole generates the equiangular spiral \(r = a e^{b\theta}\) without ever altering the organism’s shape. See Universal Golden Gnomon & Dioptra Mountain Tunnel and the geometor/divine and geometor/phyllotaxis repositories.

3. Dioptra Chapter 15, Cartesian Coordinates & the Tunnel of Samos¶

In History of Greek Mathematics (1921, Vol. II, pp. 346–351), Thomas Heath highlighted Dioptra Chapter 15 alongside Dioptra Chapter 34 (which determines the great-circle terrestrial distance between Rome and Alexandria from observations of a single lunar eclipse!). Nearly six centuries before Hero, Eupalinus of Megara excavated the \(1{,}036\)-meter Tunnel of Samos (c. 530 BCE) under Mount Castro from both ends simultaneously. Hero’s Dioptra Ch. 15 demonstrates the exact Hellenistic perfection of two-portal tunneling: rather than climbing over a rugged mountain ridge (where slope errors accumulate rapidly), Hero wraps an orthogonal horizontal Cartesian coordinate frame (\(\sum \Delta x, \sum \Delta y\)) around the level foot of the mountain, transforming physical obstacles into pure Euclidean similarity ([VI.4]).
