Book II Preface: Eudoxus & Archimedes in Stereometry

Book II, Prooemium (Schöne pp. 90–94; fols. 87r–88r). Having set forth in the First Book the mensuration of planar and curved surfaces, Hero opens the Second Book on solid bodies (στερεὰ σώματα) with a classic historical tribute: before Eudoxus of Cnidus, geometers did not possess rigorous proofs that every cylinder is triple the cone on the same base and of equal height ([XII.10]), nor that every prism is triple the pyramid; and after Eudoxus established those theorems by exhaustion, Archimedes surpassed all predecessors by proving that every sphere is two-thirds of its circumscribing cylinder (Sphere and Cylinder I.34).
