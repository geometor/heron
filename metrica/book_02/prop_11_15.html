Props. II.11–II.15: Spheres, Dionysodorus’s Torus & Archimedes’ Method Vaults¶

f. Schöne 120 · p. 120

  • Propositions II.11–II.12 (Spheres, Spherical Segments & Vaulted Basins, Schöne pp. 120–126): By Archimedes' Sphere and Cylinder I.34, the sphere is \(\frac{2}{3}\) of the circumscribing cylinder (\(\frac{\pi}{4} d^2 \cdot d\)), giving \(V_{\text{sphere}} = \frac{\pi}{6} d^3 \approx \frac{11}{21} d^3\). For a spherical segment of base radius \(r_b\) and sagitta (height) \(h\) (Prop. II.12), Hero applies Sphere and Cylinder II.2 to derive the intrinsic formula requiring no separate lookup of the full sphere’s radius:

    \[V_{\text{seg}} = \frac{1}{2}\pi r_b^2 h + \frac{1}{6}\pi h^3 = \frac{\pi}{6} h \left(3 r_b^2 + h^2\right).\]
f. Schöne 126 · p. 126

Proposition II.13 — Dionysodorus’s Torus Theorem (*Περὶ τῆς σπείρας*, Schöne pp. 126–130; fols. 95v–96r):

Hero next preserves our sole surviving Greek witness to the lost treatise On the Torus (Περὶ τῆς σπείρας) by Dionysodorus of Caunus (fl. c. 200 BCE):

“Let it be required to measure a torus (:term:`speira`), having first set out its generation. Let there be in a plane a straight line AB and two points B, C (Hero’s \(\Gamma\) ) upon it. Let the circle BCDE be perpendicular to the underlying plane in which AB lies; and while point A remains fixed, let AB be carried round in the plane until it is restored to the same position, carrying round with it the circle BCDE remaining always perpendicular to the underlying plane. Then the circumference BCDE will generate a certain surface which they call spiric (toroidal)…”

(Diagram Mapping: A = fixed center of revolution \((0,0)\); C = inner rim \(\Gamma = (8,0)\); Z = generating circle center \((14,0)\); B = outer rim \((20,0)\); D, E = vertical diameter endpoints \((14, \pm 6)\); H1, H2 = endpoints \(H, \Theta\) on the vertical axis of revolution).

  1. Dionysodorus’s Proportion: It was proved by Dionysodorus in On the Torus that as the generating circle (ZB) is to half the meridian parallelogram DEH2H1 (which has height \(\Delta E = 2r\) and width AZ \(= R\), so half its area is \(R r\)), so is the torus generated by (ZB) to the cylinder whose axis is \(H\Theta = 2r\) and whose base radius is AZ \(= R\) (volume \(\pi R^2 \cdot 2r = 2\pi R^2 r\)):

    \[\frac{V_{\text{torus}}}{2\pi R^2 r} = \frac{\pi r^2}{R r} \qquad \Longrightarrow \qquad V_{\text{torus}} = 2\pi^2 R r^2.\]

  2. Hero’s Unrolled-Cylinder Centroid Demonstration: Hero immediately adds a breathtaking physical-geometric alternative («δυνατὸν δέ ἐστι καὶ ἄλλως μετρῆσαι») that directly anticipates Pappus’s Centroid Theorem (Synagoge VII): For \(AC = 8\) and \(BC = 12\) (so minor radius ZB \(r = 6\) and major radius AZ \(R = 14\)), the circle swept by center Z has diameter \(2R = 28\) and circumference \(2\pi R \approx \frac{22}{7} \times 28 = 88\). “When therefore the torus is unrolled and becomes like a cylinder” (ἁπλωθεῖσα ἄρα ἡ σπεῖρα καὶ γενομένη ὡς κύλινδρος), it has length \(L = 88\) and circular cross-section of diameter \(BC = 12\) (area \(\frac{11}{14} \times 144 = \frac{792}{7}\)), yielding the exact rational volume:

    \[V_{\text{torus}} = 88 \times \frac{792}{7} = \frac{69{,}696}{7} = 9956\frac{4}{7}.\]
f. Schöne 130 · p. 130

  • Propositions II.14–II.15 (Archimedes’ *Method* Solids: Cylindrical Ungula & Bicylinder Groin Vault, Schöne pp. 130–132; fols. 96v–97r): Immediately after Dionysodorus’s torus, Hero cites the two celebrated final propositions of Archimedes' Method (Ἐφοδικόν)—the very theorems whose Greek leaves were torn from or barely legible at the end of the Archimedes Palimpsest!

    • Prop. II.14 (Cylindrical Hoof / Ungula): When a plane cuts a right cylinder through a diameter of its base and meets the opposite generator at height \(h\)—useful for fluted niches and half-vaults—the cut-off wedge is exactly one-sixth of the circumscribing square prism (\(d \times d \times h\)):

      \[V_{\text{ungula}} = \frac{1}{6} d^2 h = \frac{2}{3} r^2 h.\]

    • Prop. II.15 (Intersection of Two Perpendicular Equal Cylinders — Groin Vault / Steinmetz Solid): When two equal right circular cylinders of diameter \(d\) intersect at right angles inside a cube of edge \(d\)—the monumental Roman cross-vault (καμάρα)—their common solid is two-thirds of the cube:

      \[V_{\text{bicylinder}} = \frac{2}{3} d^3 = \frac{16}{3} r^3.\]
