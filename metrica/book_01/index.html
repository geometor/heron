Prooemium: Egyptian Origins & Orthogonal Unit Measures

Book I, Prooemium (Schöne pp. 2–6; fols. 67r–68r). Original geometry, just as the ancient account teaches us, was occupied with the measurements and distributions of land-parcels in Egypt—whence indeed it was called geometry (land-measurement). Necessity itself furnished human beings with the invention of the subject: for since the Nile inundated the plains of Egypt year by year, obliterating boundaries, sweeping away many plots, and augmenting others with alluvial deposits, it came to pass that the holdings of some proprietors were diminished while those of others were enlarged, so that each person could no longer recover an equal share without some exact method.

Props. I.1–I.7: Rectangles, Right/Isosceles Triangles & General Altitude Projections

Proposition I.1 (Oblong Rectangle, Schöne pp. 6–8): Given an oblong rectangular region \(AB\Gamma\Delta\) with \(AB = 5\) feet and \(B\Gamma = 3\) feet, dividing each side into unit-foot segments partitions the rectangle into \(5 \times 3 = 15\) orthogonal unit squares. Thus the area of every rectangle equals the product of two containing sides.

Props. I.10–I.16: Trapezia, Rhombi & General Quadrilaterals

Having mastered every species of triangle in Propositions I.1–I.9, Hero points out a foundational distinction between triangles and four-sided figures: whereas three given sides uniquely determine the angles and area of a triangle ([I.8]), four given sides do not determine a quadrilateral unless either a right angle, a parallel relation, or a diagonal is also specified.

Props. I.17–I.25: Regular Polygons (3-gon to 12-gon) & Golden Section Pentagon Lemma

In Propositions I.17–I.25, Hero systematically computes the area \(A_n\) of every regular \(n\)-gon of given side \(a\) from the equilateral triangle (\(n = 3\)) through the regular dodecagon (\(n = 12\)), expressing each ratio \(A_n : a^2\) both by an exact geometric construction and by a close rational multiplier suitable for architectural and surveying calculations.

Props. I.26–I.33: Circles, Archimedes’ Plinthides, Annuli & Circular Segments

Metrica Book I — Scholarly Commentary, Historical Loci & Demonstrations

The 1896 Constantinople Rediscovery & Archimedian Attribution of I.8: For over a millennium, Hero's Metrica was known only through fragmentary citations in Eutocius (In Archimedis Dim. Circ.), until Richard Schöne and Paul Tannery identified the complete three-book treatise in 1896 in the 11th-century Codex Constantinopolitanus Palatii Veteris no. 1 (fols. 67r–107v), edited by Hermann Schöne in 1903. Medieval Arabic geometer al-Bīrūnī reports that the triangle area theorem \(\Delta = \sqrt{s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c)}\) originated with Archimedes, while Metrica I.8 preserves its oldest extant Greek synthetic proof—duplicated verbatim in Hero’s Dioptra Ch. 30.
