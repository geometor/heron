Summary — Metrica Book III (Preface & Props. 1–23)
==================================================

*Structural Overview of Proportional Divisions of Planar Estates & Three-Dimensional Solids*

1. Deductive Architecture of *Metrica* Book III
-----------------------------------------------

Whereas Books I and II compute a scalar measure (area or volume) from given dimensions, **Book III solves the inverse constructive problem**: given a figure and a target ratio :math:`m : n`, construct the dividing line or plane with zero geometric error (*"not exceeding or falling short by a single millet-seed"*):

1. **Philosophical Prooemium**: Grounds cadastral division in classical Greek ethics—superposition (*ἐφαρμογή*) establishes arithmetic equality, geometric :term:`proportion <analogia>` realizes distributive justice (*δικαιοσύνη*), and Euclidean proof renders the boundary indisputable.
2. **Triangle Divisions (Props. III.1–III.3)**: Divides any triangle in ratio :math:`m : n` from a vertex (**III.1**, linear base division), parallel to a base (**III.2**, duplicate ratio / mean-proportional quadrance :math:`AD^2 = \frac{m}{m+n} AB^2`), or from an arbitrary side point (**III.3**, reciprocal side product).
3. **Quadrilaterals, Polygons, Annuli & Exterior Points (Props. III.4–III.19)**: Solves parallel trapezium cuts via root-mean-square quadrance (**III.5**), concentric circle cuts (**III.9**), and straight-line cuts constrained to pass through an **exterior landmark** (**III.10–III.13**).
4. **Stereometric Divisions & Cube-Root Extraction (Props. III.20–III.23)**: Divides pyramids, cones, and frusta by parallel planes via triplicate ratios (**III.20–III.22**), introduces **Hero's cube-root algorithm** (:math:`\sqrt[3]{100} \approx 4\frac{9}{14}`), and concludes with :person:`Dionysodorus's <dionysodorus>` conic solution for dividing a sphere (**III.23**).

--------------------------------------------

     - Exact Constructive Law
     - *Metrica* III Exemplar
   * - **1D / Common-Altitude** (III.1)

   * - **3D Pyramid / Cone** (III.20)
     - :math:`x^3 = \lambda\,L^3`
     - :math:`\frac{4}{5} \times 125 = 100 \implies x \approx 4\frac{9}{14}`
   * - **3D Frustum** (III.21–22)
     - :math:`x^3 = (1 - \lambda)a^3 + \lambda b^3`
     - Weighted mean of base cubes
